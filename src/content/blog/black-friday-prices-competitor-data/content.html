Why discounting without market data costs margin

The most common Black Friday mistake is treating a competitor's headline discount as a command. A rival advertises 30% off, so the store applies 30% off across the catalog. That ignores whether the products are comparable, whether the rival raised its reference price, and whether shoppers are actually choosing on price.

Imagine a $100 product with a $45 landed cost. Before payment fees, shipping support, returns, and acquisition costs, a 20% discount leaves $35 of gross dollars. A 30% discount leaves $25. The second markdown is only 10 percentage points deeper, but it removes nearly 29% of the remaining gross dollars.

Shopify’s pricing guidance recommends balancing competitive pricing with costs, customer value, and market context. A competitor’s price tells you what shoppers can see, not their margin, shipping subsidy, inventory position, or acquisition economics.

The goal is not to be the cheapest store. Be compelling where comparison is intense, while preserving full price where your product or service earns a premium.

A data-led framework for Black Friday prices

1. Build a comparable competitor set

Start with three to five competitors that sell the same type of product to the same customer. Separate direct product matches from substitutes. A premium wool coat should not be benchmarked against a fast-fashion polyester coat simply because both appear for “winter coat.”

For each competitor, record the regular price, sale price, code or automatic promotion, shipping cost, free-shipping threshold, stock status, available variants, and bundle incentives. Add product reviews, materials, dimensions, and warranty so you are comparing the complete offer.

EcomScout’s Shopify store research platform can help surface bestsellers, product moves, and daily price changes. Use it to find the competitors worth watching and establish a baseline before Black Friday—not to copy every number you see.

Create a simple price index for each comparable item: Your comparable price ÷ median comparable price × 100. An index of 100 means you are at the market median; 95 means you are about 5% below it. Add a column for your value difference: delivery, materials, reviews, warranty, or bundle.

2. Find bestsellers and recent price changes

Do not spend equal attention on every SKU. Pull your own last-90-day sales, conversion rate, gross margin, stock cover, return rate, and review strength. Then compare the short list with competitor bestsellers and recent changes.

  • Several competitors discount the same product family: demand is likely price-sensitive.
  • One competitor drops price while going out of stock: do not chase a temporary signal.
  • A competitor raises price before the weekend: the market may support a smaller discount.
  • A bestseller stays at full price: value, availability, or trust may matter more than markdown depth.

Keep a dated snapshot. One observation is a data point; four weeks of observations are a trend. Confirm MAP, wholesale, and regional pricing constraints before reacting.

Track meaningful competitor price and stock changes so you can respond without chasing every alert.

3. Decide what to discount, by how much, and what to protect

Give every product one role. A four-bucket model keeps a Shopify pricing strategy 2026 plan practical:

Product roleSignalTypical action
Traffic driverHigh demand, high comparison, healthy stockMatch or slightly beat the market range, within your floor
Margin keeperStrong conversion, differentiated product, thin stockSmall discount or full price
Inventory moverAging stock, high cover, seasonal relevanceDeeper markdown if the floor still works
Full-price anchorNew launch, premium hero, or already-selling SKUKeep full price; add value with service or bundle

Calculate a floor for each SKU before choosing the offer. Include product cost, payment fees, packaging, expected returns, shipping subsidy, channel fees, and the acquisition cost you are willing to carry. If a discount falls below the floor, change the offer instead: use a bundle, free-shipping threshold, gift, or targeted segment.

Use competitor data to choose the position inside the safe range. If comparable products sell at $72, $75, and $79, a $74 price may be competitive without matching the lowest offer. If your product has better reviews and faster delivery, $79 with a clear value message may be stronger.

4. Turn the decisions into pricing rules

Write rules in plain language before editing the catalog:

  • For winter-basics, reduce price by 15%, but never below $42.
  • For bfcm-traffic, set a fixed price from the approved price sheet.
  • For products with more than 90 days of cover, reduce by 25% only when the margin floor still works.
  • Exclude new arrivals, low-stock variants, subscriptions, and wholesale price lists.

Use one rule per product role. Preview the SKU and variant count, check rounding, and compare the result with the approved price sheet. Record original prices, timing, owner, and rollback method. The campaign should be repeatable, auditable, and narrow enough to fix if a condition changes.

Schedule the campaign and check for conflicts

Create the Black Friday job a few days before launch, but leave it paused until final review. Check for conflicts with automatic discounts, discount codes, subscription or customer-specific pricing, wholesale and regional price lists, bundles, free-shipping thresholds, inventory rules, and campaign copy that promises a different price.

Run a small test. Confirm the storefront price, compare-at display, cart behavior, checkout total, and connected feeds. Then schedule the broader job with the correct timezone and end time.

Watch competitor price moves during the weekend

Do not react to every notification. Review once daily in the lead-up, then two or three times during the weekend for hero SKUs and high-spend campaigns.

  • If the median competitor price changes less than 5%, hold your price.
  • If two or more direct competitors move 5–10% lower and conversion falls, test a targeted response.
  • If a competitor drops below your floor, protect margin and emphasize value instead of matching.
  • If a competitor sells out, stop chasing the old price and review your stock position.

Log the move, response, and result. The useful output is a better rule for the next campaign.

Roll back cleanly when the sale ends

Rollback should be planned, not a late-night cleanup. Before launch, save the original price for every affected variant and note exclusions. Set the end time, owner, and verification checklist in the campaign record.

  1. Run the rollback from the saved original-price snapshot.
  2. Confirm a sample from every product role and market.
  3. Check that compare-at prices, badges, ads, and email banners no longer promise an expired deal.
  4. Export units, revenue, contribution margin, AOV, conversion rate, and returns.
  5. Keep the competitor snapshot and decision log for the next event.

That prevents two expensive errors: leaving a deep discount live too long and restoring the wrong price after multiple edits.

FAQ

How should I set Black Friday prices for my Shopify store?
Set Black Friday prices by combining comparable competitor offers with a product-level margin floor, inventory position, and value difference. Use competitor prices to choose your market position, not to decide the discount automatically.

What is a good Black Friday discount percentage?
There is no safe universal percentage. A good discount is the deepest offer that stays above your contribution-margin floor and fits the product’s role.

How do I monitor competitor prices during Black Friday?
Monitor a focused set of direct competitors and record price, promotion, shipping, stock, and date. EcomScout can help surface bestsellers and price changes so you can review meaningful moves instead of manually checking every store.

How can I roll back Shopify prices after a sale?
Save the original variant prices before applying the sale, then restore that snapshot after the campaign. A bulk pricing tool with price history and rollback reduces manual re-entry, but you should still verify representative products, markets, and storefront displays.

Wrapping up

The best Black Friday prices are not the lowest prices in your category. They respond to market pressure while protecting store economics.
