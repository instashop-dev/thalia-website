Shopify merchants increasingly send shoppers to destinations outside their own checkout: Amazon, eBay, Walmart, supplier sites, partner retailers, and brand-owned stores. That creates a practical challenge. The sale may happen somewhere else, but the click still needs to be clear, compliant, and measurable.

A well-designed Shopify external link button gives shoppers an honest next step and gives operators a usable attribution trail. The button is only the visible part of the system. The important work is mapping the right destination, writing accurate CTA copy, preserving affiliate parameters, and checking what happens after the redirect.

Why off-site product journeys break so easily

An affiliate or multi-channel storefront can look like a normal Shopify store. It has collections, product pages, search, and editorial content. The important difference is what happens after a visitor clicks the main call to action. Instead of adding the item to a Shopify cart, the visitor may need to continue to an external retailer or a brand-owned checkout.

That handoff is where many stores lose clarity. A generic “Buy now” button can make shoppers wonder whether they are still on the same site. A hidden or stale destination can send traffic to the wrong retailer. A product imported from a marketplace may have a different title, price, availability, or variation. And if the link is not tagged or recorded, the operator sees a pageview but cannot tell which product generated the referral.

Shopify supports external links in content, and its Buy Button channel is designed for placing Shopify products on non-Shopify websites. Those are useful tools, but they solve a different problem from managing a catalog whose primary purchase action lives elsewhere. The operator still needs a repeatable way to connect each product to the right destination.

The business case is also bigger than a button. An affiliate link is the bridge between your content and the partner’s conversion system. If that bridge is unclear, you risk lower trust, broken attribution, and avoidable compliance issues. A clear external CTA makes the handoff understandable before the shopper clicks.

How to set up a Shopify external link button

Step 1: Decide what the click should accomplish

Start with the customer journey, not the button color. For each product type, answer one question: where should a ready-to-buy visitor go next?

  • Affiliate product: send the visitor to the partner retailer using the approved affiliate URL.
  • Comparison product: show two or more retailer options, with honest labels such as “Buy on Amazon” or “Compare at Walmart.”
  • Supplier or dropship product: send the visitor to the fulfillment partner only when that destination is part of your operating model.
  • Hybrid catalog: keep “Add to cart” for products sold directly and show an external CTA for products fulfilled elsewhere.

The answer determines whether you need one link per product or multiple options per product. It also determines the copy. “View retailer options” sets a different expectation from “Buy from our store.” Clear copy is a conversion feature because it removes surprise at the moment of handoff.

Before publishing, check the partner program terms. Some programs restrict paid search, coupon sites, direct linking, link cloaking, or the use of certain creative. A technically working URL can still be a poor business choice if it violates the program’s rules.

Do not assume that a product title is enough to identify a destination. Variants, pack sizes, colors, and regional storefronts can all lead to different URLs. For a catalog with hundreds of products, manual copying creates a quiet but expensive risk: one character can change the destination while the button still looks normal.

If you are building your own workflow, import links from a CSV and validate the hostname. If you use an app, look for product-level mapping, bulk editing, and active-link reporting. Those controls turn link management into an operating process instead of a one-time theme edit.

Step 3: Write a CTA that explains the handoff

Your Shopify CTA button should answer three questions quickly:

  1. Where am I going? Use the retailer, marketplace, or destination name when appropriate.
  2. What happens next? “View on retailer” and “Shop the supplier” are more precise than a vague “Continue.”
  3. Who handles the transaction? If checkout, delivery, returns, or payment are handled by another company, disclose that in nearby copy.

Good CTA examples include:

  • Buy on Amazon
  • View at Walmart
  • See retailer options
  • Shop this product on the partner site
  • Check availability at the manufacturer

Keep the button visually prominent, but do not make the external destination look like a Shopify checkout if it is not one. Use a short explanation below the CTA: “You’ll complete your purchase on Amazon” or “Prices and availability are set by the retailer.” That small sentence helps protect trust.

If the link opens a new tab, say so only when it helps the user. Test that the destination works on mobile and preserves the affiliate parameter.

Step 4: Add measurement before you publish

UTMs answer “where did the visitor come from?” They do not replace the affiliate network’s own tracking ID. Use both when the partner program allows it: the affiliate parameter for commission attribution and UTMs for your analytics reporting.

Then test the complete path in an incognito browser:

  • Click from the article, social post, or product page.
  • Confirm the destination loads and the affiliate parameter remains intact.
  • Check that redirects do not strip campaign values.
  • Check GA4 Realtime or DebugView for the session and campaign dimensions.
  • Record the test date and destination status.

UTMs on a Shopify App Store install link help measure the click into the listing. They do not automatically reveal whether the visitor installed the app unless the app ecosystem or your attribution setup passes that event into a shared reporting system. Treat app installs as a separate conversion KPI.

Common mistakes that quietly cost affiliate stores revenue

The most expensive failures are usually operational, not visual. They happen when the button is attractive but the destination, disclosure, or attribution is wrong.

Disclosure matters too. If you may earn a commission, say so in clear language near the recommendation. The goal is not to add legal noise to every button. It is to help the visitor understand the relationship before clicking.

Measurement is just as practical. Traffic can fall into “direct / none” when campaign links lack UTM parameters or when redirects strip them. A store can therefore receive qualified traffic while the report makes it look unattributed. Check tagged links and redirect behavior whenever source information is missing.

FAQ

How do I add an external link button to a Shopify product page?

Add a destination URL to the product, choose clear CTA text, and display the button in the product-page template. For a catalog with many links, use a link-management workflow or app so you can map, update, and audit destinations without editing theme code product by product.

Can I use affiliate links on Shopify?

Yes, when the affiliate program allows your promotion method and you disclose the relationship clearly. Keep the affiliate ID intact, use honest button copy, and verify the destination for each product or variant.

How do I track external link clicks in GA4?

Use consistent UTM parameters on links that lead to your site or campaign landing pages, and configure click events where the outbound interaction happens. GA4 can report manual source, medium, campaign, and content values, but affiliate-network conversions may require a separate partner dashboard or integration.

Should a Shopify external link button open in a new tab?

There is no universal best choice. Test the behavior on mobile and desktop, make the destination clear, and avoid interrupting the shopper with unexpected windows. The key requirement is a fast, trustworthy handoff with preserved tracking parameters.

Wrapping up

A strong off-site shopping journey is a small system, not just a button. Decide where each product should send the shopper, map links at the right level, label the CTA honestly, disclose affiliate relationships, and test the full redirect. Then use one UTM convention so analytics can distinguish blog, social, and creative performance.
