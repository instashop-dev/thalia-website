Why agentic commerce makes catalog hygiene a growth task

Traditional search often gives a shopper a list of pages. An AI shopping assistant may give the shopper a much shorter recommendation set. It has to decide which products match the request, explain the trade-offs, and answer follow-up questions such as “Is this available in medium?” or “Does it arrive before Friday?”

That changes the cost of vague product data. A title like “Classic Tee” does not tell an AI agent the material, fit, audience, color, or use case. A variant named “Option 3” hides the choice a buyer needs to compare.

Shopify’s own agentic commerce guide describes this shift as AI helping shoppers browse, compare, and purchase in a conversation. Shopify also says its Catalog provides structured product information for AI surfaces. That is useful infrastructure, but it does not remove the merchant’s responsibility to keep source data accurate.

Think of the catalog as a set of answers: what it is, who it is for, what makes it different, which options exist, whether it is available, and what happens after purchase. Conflicting answers reduce recommendation confidence.

Agentic commerce Shopify: a four-step workflow

Step 1: Write the questions your catalog must answer

Start with shopper questions, not columns. Collect 10 to 20 real questions from support tickets, site search, sales calls, and product reviews.

Group them into four sets:

Shopper questionProduct data neededTypical failure
“What is this made from?”Material, composition, careInformation exists only in an image or PDF
“Which option fits me?”Size, dimensions, compatibility, variant labelsVariants use internal codes
“Can I get it this week?”Inventory, location, processing timeStock and delivery promises disagree
“Is this right for my use case?”Use case, features, category, audienceDescription is creative but not specific

This gives your team a testable definition of completeness. Use the same question set for one collection first, then apply the model to the rest of the catalog.

Step 2: Build a repeatable Shopify product data export

Create an export that includes the product and variant identifiers needed to trace every row back to the source. At minimum, include title, handle, description, vendor, product type, tags, images, SKU, barcode, price, compare-at price, inventory, variant options, and status.

Then add the fields that answer your shopper questions. This may include Shopify metafields, product taxonomy, material, care instructions, compatibility, dimensions, shipping notes, and structured product attributes.

  1. Export stable IDs. Titles change. Product and variant IDs make comparisons reliable.
  2. Keep variants visible. A parent product can look complete while one important size or color is missing an image, price, or inventory value.
  3. Save the export definition. A repeatable field set makes the next review comparable with the last one.

If your catalog is large, filter the first export by collection, product type, or last updated date. A focused file is easier to inspect than a full database dump.

Step 3: Run a catalog preflight before distribution

Treat the export like a release candidate. Do not edit blindly in a spreadsheet and upload it without a review trail. First, find rows that can create a poor recommendation or a broken purchase path.

Check for:

  • Missing or duplicate SKUs and variant IDs.
  • Titles that omit the product type or the main differentiator.
  • Variant names that make sense only to the internal team.
  • Empty material, size, compatibility, or care fields where shoppers need them.
  • Images that do not match the selected color or variant.
  • Prices, compare-at prices, and inventory values that conflict with the storefront.
  • Metafields with different units, spellings, or capitalization for the same attribute.

Assign every check one of three states: pass, needs decision, or block. “Needs decision” is important. Some missing fields are acceptable for a product; others should stop distribution until an owner fixes them.

Do not turn the process into a demand for longer descriptions. A concise, specific product record is more useful than a paragraph that buries the decisive attribute.

Step 4: Measure discovery quality and keep the source current

After cleanup, record a baseline. Track AI referral sessions where analytics exposes them, product impressions, product-page clicks, conversion rate, and support questions about missing information. Do not claim that a catalog export caused a lift unless your measurement setup can support that conclusion.

Review high-value collections more often than the long tail. Re-export after a pricing change, assortment change, or inventory migration.

Common catalog mistakes that weaken AI discovery

1. Treating the product description as the whole catalog

Important facts often live in metafields, variant options, images, PDFs, or support documents. Export the fields that carry meaning, then decide which information belongs in the main product record.

2. Fixing the parent product but missing the variant rows

AI shopping depends on the option the customer can actually buy. If “Black / Medium” has no image or inventory while the parent product looks complete, the record is still incomplete.

3. Using internal language in customer-facing fields

“BLK-M” may be useful as a SKU. It is not a useful shopper-facing variant label. Keep the internal identifier, but give the customer a descriptive option name.

4. Confusing structured data with keyword stuffing

Adding the phrase “AI shopping Shopify” to every title will not make a catalog useful. The goal of structured data on Shopify is to make facts clear and machine-readable. Use natural language and accurate attributes.

5. Never reviewing the export after a bulk change

Catalog quality decays. A new supplier feed, theme migration, translation, or bulk edit can overwrite fields that looked correct last quarter. Keep a dated export and compare changes before the next major campaign.
