# How to Set Your Black Friday Prices Using Competitor Data

Black Friday prices are easier to set when you can see what the market is doing—but competitor data should be a guardrail, not your entire pricing strategy. Shopify merchants generated $14.6 billion during BFCM 2025, up 27% from the year before. When demand and competition peak together, a few rushed percentage edits can erase the profit from your best weekend.

The practical answer is to combine competitor price history with your costs, inventory, product role, and margin floor. Then turn the decisions into scheduled rules you can review, monitor, and reverse.

## 1. Why discounting without market data costs margin

The most common Black Friday mistake is treating a competitor's headline discount as a command. A rival advertises 30% off, so the store applies 30% off across the catalog. That sounds responsive, but it ignores whether the products are comparable, whether the rival raised its reference price, and whether shoppers are actually choosing on price.

Imagine a $100 product with a $45 landed cost. Before payment fees, shipping support, returns, and acquisition costs, a 20% discount leaves $35 of gross dollars. A 30% discount leaves $25. The second markdown is only 10 percentage points deeper, but it removes nearly 29% of the remaining gross dollars. If the extra volume does not make up the difference, revenue rises while contribution profit falls.

Shopify’s pricing guidance makes the same point: competitive pricing should be balanced with costs, customer value, and market context. A competitor’s price tells you what shoppers can see, not their margin, shipping subsidy, inventory position, or acquisition economics.

The goal is not to be the cheapest store. Be compelling where comparison is intense, while preserving full price where your product or service earns a premium.

## 2. A data-led framework for Black Friday prices

### Step 1 — Build a comparable competitor set

Start with three to five competitors that sell the same type of product to the same customer. Separate direct product matches from substitutes. A premium wool coat should not be benchmarked against a fast-fashion polyester coat simply because both appear for “winter coat.”

For each competitor, record:

- Regular price and current sale price
- Discount code or automatic promotion
- Shipping cost and free-shipping threshold
- Stock status and available variants
- Bundle, gift, or loyalty incentives
- Product reviews, materials, dimensions, and warranty

This is where [EcomScout’s Shopify store research platform](https://ecomscout.com/products/analytics-platform?utm_source=blog&utm_medium=organic&utm_campaign=black-friday-prices-competitor-data&utm_content=ecomscout-research-link) helps. Use its store and product data to surface bestsellers, product moves, and daily price changes. Establish a baseline before Black Friday; do not copy every number you see.

Create a simple price index for each comparable item:

`Your comparable price ÷ median comparable price × 100`

An index of 100 means you are at the market median; 95 means you are about 5% below it. Add a column for your value difference: delivery, materials, reviews, warranty, or bundle.

### Step 2 — Find bestsellers and recent price changes

Do not spend equal attention on every SKU. Pull your own last-90-day sales, conversion rate, gross margin, stock cover, return rate, and customer-review strength. Then compare the short list with competitor bestsellers and recent changes.

Look for patterns:

- Several competitors discount the same product family: demand is likely price-sensitive.
- One competitor drops price while going out of stock: do not chase a temporary signal.
- A competitor raises price before the weekend: the market may support a smaller discount than expected.
- A bestseller stays at full price: value, availability, or brand trust may matter more than markdown depth.

Keep a dated snapshot. One observation is a data point; four weeks of observations are a trend. Confirm MAP, wholesale, and regional pricing constraints before reacting.

### Step 3 — Decide what to discount, by how much, and what to protect

Give every product one role. A four-bucket model keeps a **Shopify pricing strategy 2026** plan practical:

| Product role | Signal | Typical action |
|---|---|---|
| Traffic driver | High demand, high comparison, healthy stock | Match or slightly beat the relevant market range, within your floor |
| Margin keeper | Strong conversion, differentiated product, thin stock | Small discount or full price |
| Inventory mover | Aging stock, high cover, seasonal relevance | Deeper markdown if the floor still works |
| Full-price anchor | New launch, premium hero, or already-selling SKU | Keep full price; add value with service, bundle, or gift |

Calculate a floor for each SKU before you choose the offer. Include product cost, payment fees, packaging, expected returns, shipping subsidy, marketplace or channel fees, and the acquisition cost you are willing to carry. If a discount falls below the floor, change the offer rather than forcing the price: use a bundle, free shipping threshold, gift with purchase, or a targeted customer segment.

Use competitor data to choose the position inside the safe range. For example, if three comparable products sell at $72, $75, and $79, a $74 price may be competitive without automatically matching the lowest offer. If your product has better reviews and faster delivery, $79 with a clear value message may be the stronger decision.

### Step 4 — Turn the decisions into pricing rules

Write rules in plain language before you edit the catalog. Examples:

- “For collection `winter-basics`, reduce price by 15%, but never below $42.”
- “For SKUs tagged `bfcm-traffic`, set a fixed price from the approved price sheet.”
- “For products with more than 90 days of cover and a margin floor above $20, reduce by 25%.”
- “Exclude new arrivals, low-stock variants, subscriptions, and wholesale price lists.”

Use one rule per product role. Preview the SKU and variant count, check rounding, and compare the result with the approved price sheet. Record original prices, timing, owner, and rollback method.

This is the operational heart of **schedule discounts Shopify** stores often need: the campaign should be repeatable, auditable, and narrow enough to fix if a condition changes.

## 3. Common mistakes that turn a good sale into a weak one

Shopify reported that BFCM 2025 produced $14.6 billion in merchant sales, with an average cart price of $114.70. That scale creates opportunity, but it also makes operational errors expensive. Shopify’s pricing strategy guidance recommends starting with a margin floor, testing changes on a smaller set, and monitoring conversion, net sales, AOV, and returning-customer rate after a price change.

| Mistake | What it costs | Better control |
|---|---|---|
| Matching the lowest competitor blindly | Margin disappears on products that did not need a deeper offer | Use the median market price and your value difference |
| Discounting the whole catalog | Full-price demand is subsidized by unnecessary markdowns | Segment by product role and inventory need |
| Ignoring shipping and returns | The displayed margin is higher than the contribution margin | Include landed and post-purchase costs in the floor |
| Editing without a snapshot | A second edit can compound the first discount | Save original prices and use one controlled job per rule |
| Treating one price check as truth | A temporary stockout or coupon creates a false signal | Track a dated baseline and meaningful changes |

Also check local reference-price requirements. If you show a compare-at price or “was/now” claim, make sure the reference price is accurate and defensible. Shoppers should understand the offer before checkout.

## 4. Schedule the campaign and check for conflicts

Create the Black Friday job a few days before launch, but leave it paused until final review. Check for conflicts with:

- Existing automatic discounts and discount codes
- Subscription or customer-specific pricing
- Wholesale, Markets, and regional price lists
- Bundles, gift-with-purchase offers, and free-shipping thresholds
- Inventory rules that hide or disable products
- Email, ad, feed, and merchandising copy that promises a different price

Run a small test. Confirm the storefront price, compare-at display, cart behavior, checkout total, and connected feeds. Then schedule the broader job with the correct timezone and end time.

## 5. Watch competitor price moves during the weekend

Do not react to every notification. Review once daily in the lead-up, then two or three times during the weekend for hero SKUs and high-spend campaigns.

Use action thresholds:

- If the median competitor price changes less than 5%, hold your price.
- If two or more direct competitors move 5–10% lower and your conversion rate falls, test a targeted response.
- If a competitor drops below your floor, protect margin and emphasize value rather than matching.
- If a competitor sells out, stop chasing the old price and review your stock position.

Log the move, response, and result. The useful output is a better rule for the next campaign.

## 6. Roll back cleanly when the sale ends

Rollback should be planned, not a late-night cleanup. Before launch, save the original price for every affected variant and note exclusions. Set the end time, owner, and verification checklist in the campaign record.

When the sale ends:

1. Run the rollback from the saved original-price snapshot.
2. Confirm a sample from every product role and market.
3. Check that compare-at prices, badges, ads, and email banners no longer promise an expired deal.
4. Export units, revenue, contribution margin, AOV, conversion rate, and returns.
5. Keep the competitor snapshot and decision log for the next event.

That prevents two expensive errors: leaving a deep discount live too long and restoring the wrong price after multiple edits.

## FAQ

**Q: How should I set Black Friday prices for my Shopify store?**

Set Black Friday prices by combining comparable competitor offers with a product-level margin floor, inventory position, and value difference. Use competitor prices to choose your market position, not to decide the discount automatically.

**Q: What is a good Black Friday discount percentage?**

There is no safe universal percentage. A good discount is the deepest offer that stays above your contribution-margin floor and fits the product’s role; that may be 10% for a hero product, 20% for a traffic driver, or more for aging inventory.

**Q: How do I monitor competitor prices during Black Friday?**

Monitor a focused set of direct competitors and record price, promotion, shipping, stock, and date. A research platform such as EcomScout can help surface bestsellers and price changes so you can review meaningful moves instead of manually checking every store.

**Q: How can I roll back Shopify prices after a sale?**

Save the original variant prices before applying the sale, then restore that snapshot after the campaign. A bulk pricing tool with price history and rollback can reduce manual re-entry, but you should still verify representative products, markets, and storefront displays.
